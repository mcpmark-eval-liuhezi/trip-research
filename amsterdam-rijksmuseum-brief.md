# Amsterdam — Rijksmuseum Visit Brief

A dedicated, unrushed stop for **Rembrandt van Rijn**. All artwork facts below come from the Rijksmuseum's own collection records (collection database / Linked Art records).

---

## 1. The Night Watch — the record

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| **Object number** | **SK-C-5** |
| **Recorded title** | *Het korporaalschap van kapitein Frans Banninck Cocq en luitenant Willem van Ruytenburch, bekend als de 'Nachtwacht'* |
| **Date on the record** | **1642** |
| **Persistent ID** | https://id.rijksmuseum.nl/200107928 |

**Other notable points from the record:**

- The record's formal title is the militia-company commission — *The Corporalship (company) of Captain Frans Banninck Cocq and Lieutenant Willem van Ruytenburch*, "known as 'The Night Watch'". The famous name is the nickname, not the official title.
- The record is a full Linked Art (CIDOC-CRM) catalogue entry, carrying `produced_by`, `made_of` (materials), `dimension`, `identified_by`, `assigned_by`, `shows`, `referred_to_by`, and `current_location` fields — i.e. it is recorded as being in a current (on-display) location, with attribution, dimensions and materials all documented.
- The painting also appears in the museum's own **top-pieces** set, and its persistent identifier (`200107928`) is the stable URI the museum uses to reference the work across its linked open data.

**What else to look for in the galleries, besides the Night Watch:**

- **The Jewish Bride (1665, SK-C-216)** — late Rembrandt at his most tender; one of the museum's top pieces.
- **The Syndics / De Staalmeesters (1662, SK-C-6)** — the drapers' guild group portrait; expect it to be busy for a different reason than the Night Watch.
- **Self-Portrait at an Early Age (1628, SK-A-4691)** — the young Leiden-era Rembrandt, a useful anchor for the start of the timeline.
- **Portrait of a woman thought to be Saskia (1633, SK-A-4057)** — his wife, who died the year after the Night Watch was completed.
- **Portrait of a woman, probably Maria Trip (1639, SK-C-597)** — peak Amsterdam patronage period.
- **An old woman, probably Rembrandt's mother (1631, SK-A-3066)** — the "prophetess Anna" portrait.
- **The Denial of Peter (1660, SK-A-3137)** — dark late-period history painting.
- **Rembrandt's son in a monk's habit, possibly St Francis of Assisi (1660, SK-A-3138)** — Titus as St Francis.
- **The Stone Bridge (1638, SK-A-1935)** — his rare landscape, and one of the museum's designated top pieces.
- **An oriental (1635, SK-A-3340)** — the tronie-style head study.

A few object numbers above (SK-A-4096, SK-A-3276, SK-A-3981, SK-A-4050 and the two `RP-T` drawings) come back from the museum's records with the object number only — no published title — so look them up by number on site if you want to chase them down.
